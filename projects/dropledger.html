Start where the receipt already is

The proposed workflow uses the phone’s native share sheet. A user can send a photo or PDF to DropLedger, optionally add a note such as which property it belongs to, and let the app process it.

The goal is to remove routine typing: vendor, date, amount, and category should come from the document rather than a form.

Borrow a familiar mental model

The home screen treats properties like conversation threads. Opening a property reveals its expense history, with access to search, filters, document capture, and export.

An Uncategorized thread stays at the top. When a receipt cannot confidently be connected to a property, it belongs in a visible review queue instead of being silently filed under a guess.

The proposed pipeline

  • Capture or share a document, with an optional context note.
  • Store the original receipt and send it to a multimodal extraction service.
  • Extract structured fields for the amount, date, vendor, category, and implied property.
  • Resolve the property against the user’s existing list, or mark the expense for review.
  • Export a selected date range as a CSV ledger.

Designing around uncertainty

The proposed architecture uses React Native with Expo, Supabase storage and PostgreSQL, and an Edge Function calling Gemini Flash. The important product decision is what happens when the model is unsure: the original document remains available and the expense is routed for review.

The tax-preparation output is deliberately straightforward: a dated expense ledger that can be shared with an accountant.
